The problem I solve

Most macro analysis covers the fundamentals well. Supply, valuations, policy — that work is done.

What it misses is the demand side: who owns what, how much, at what cost, and what would make them sell. In EM sovereign debt, that gap is large. The data is hard to find, harder to interpret, and most desks don't have anyone who has spent serious time building the frameworks to use it.

I have.

I've spent a decade solving a genuinely hard data problem — tracking who holds emerging market government bonds. Why? Because fund managers are constrained agents (they benchmark against an index, can't deviate too far), which means positioning becomes self-reinforcing and price moves can detach from fundamentals.

The data to understand this positioning is scattered across ~100 government websites — PDFs, Excel files, no APIs, irregular schedules, inconsistent formats. In the past I've been collecting it with a mix of humans and scrapers, stored in Postgres. That process works, but it's fragile and expensive.

What you want I can build in 30 days: an AI-native version of that collection pipeline — something that can read PDFs, navigate websites, wrangle the data, and keep a clean DB updated automatically. LLMs make this tractable now in a way it wasn't 5 years ago.

What I've done

I spent the first half of my career as a fixed income strategist — ten years in developed markets, ten in EM. My differentiator was always the same: I could find positioning data others couldn't, structure it in ways that led directly to a trade, and say clearly whether the signal said to go with the flow or against it.

In 2016 I left to test that thesis commercially. I founded TrounceFlow, a data company focused entirely on EM investor positioning. In 2023 I sold the IP to Exante Data and joined to scale it — better resources, better client network. I spent two years building the product further and learning a hard lesson: I'm excellent at generating the insight, and poor at monetising it. Selling data subscriptions to institutional clients requires a different skillset to applying that data to make money.

That's why I'm looking for a pod role. I want to be on the side of the table where the edge gets used, not sold.
