Where bond-market shocks land: yields adjust on demand, quantities on supply.

  • Sovereign bond supply is near-vertical — issuance is determined by fiscal needs (deficits, refinancing, debt management), not by yield in the short run.
  • A supply shock (e.g., unexpected deficit financing) shifts the supply curve right. Quantity rises by the size of the shift; yield rises modestly along the demand curve.
  • A demand shock (e.g., foreign investors selling) shifts the demand curve left. Yield rises sharply; quantity is essentially unchanged because supply is vertical.
  • Yields adjust on every shock; quantities only move when supply does. Quantities tell you what supply did, not what demand did.
