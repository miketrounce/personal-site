Where bond-market shocks land: yields move, quantities don't.

  • Sovereign bond supply is near-vertical — issuance is determined by fiscal needs (deficits, refinancing, debt management), not by yield in the short run.
  • A supply shock (e.g., unexpected deficit financing) shifts the vertical supply curve right. Yield rises sharply; quantity rises slightly.
  • A demand shock (e.g., foreign investors selling) shifts the upward demand curve left. Yield rises; quantity is essentially unchanged because supply is vertical.
  • In both cases, yields take most of the adjustment. Quantities mostly tell you what supply did, not what demand did.
