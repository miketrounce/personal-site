Flow of Funds Analysis

Who Buys the Bonds?

Understanding demand in emerging market sovereign debt

  • A framework for analysing EM government bond markets
  • Stock & flow: who holds the debt, and how that changes over time
  • Why the composition of demand matters more than the level of yields
The supply of bonds is nearly fixed. The interesting question is always on the demand side.
01 / The Setup

Governments borrow. Always.

Supply is quasi-fixed — the analysis lives on the demand side

  • Every government — EM and DM alike — runs a persistent fiscal deficit
  • A deficit of 3% of GDP with 3% growth keeps the debt-to-GDP ratio constant
  • Price sensitivity to borrowing costs exists but is weak — governments issue what they need
  • Supply will be issued. It must be absorbed. The question is: by whom?
  • All the analytical interest is on the demand side

03 / Home Bias

Investors stay close to home

EM participation is an active bet, not the default

Why home bias persists

  • Currency risk unhedged exposure
  • Liquidity risk exit in a crisis?
  • Benchmark constraints tracking error
  • Capital controls e.g. China

What this means for EM

  • Foreign participation active decision
  • When in they chose to be
  • When out something changed
  • Flows are informative not random
When foreigners leave an EM market, it is not noise — it is a signal about a specific calculus that shifted.
04 / The Puzzle

Foreign participation swings far too much

Markets aren't clearing the way textbooks say they should

  • South Africa foreign ownership: peaked at ~37%, then halved
  • Turkey: foreigners sold consistently for years, then reversed sharply
  • In efficient markets, yields would rise to attract buyers back — this doesn't reliably happen
  • If all markets were efficient, foreigners would hold the same proportionate share in each
  • EM bond markets are not efficient in the textbook sense — and that's what makes them interesting
05 / Why the Swings?

Three mechanisms break the feedback loop

Why yields don't automatically equilibrate foreign demand

  • Currency risk — higher yields don't compensate if the exchange rate falls faster; exits become self-reinforcing
  • Narratives & credibility — institutional risk (central bank independence, rule of law) can't be priced in a spread; the whole framework becomes suspect
  • Index mechanics — passive funds are forced buyers and sellers as country weights move; price-insensitive flows that have nothing to do with value
  • Credit rating floors, VaR limits, country caps — institutional constraints create cliff edges
  • Multiple equilibria: a market can sit in "in" or "out" for years, with narrative — not yield — as the switch
06 / The Analytical Payoff

Flows tell you what prices can't

If markets were efficient, just watch yields. They're not — so track who holds what

Stock

  • Foreigners % of total
  • Domestic banks % of total
  • Pension / insurance % of total
  • Central bank % of total

Flow

  • Foreigners Δ last quarter
  • Domestic banks Δ last quarter
  • Pension / insurance Δ last quarter
  • Central bank Δ last quarter
Stock tells you whether the holder base is stable or fragile today.
Flow tells you which direction it's moving — and who the marginal buyer is.
Together: the signal that price alone misses.
